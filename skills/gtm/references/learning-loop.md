# Learn from execution and outcomes

Start with the prior run, selected play/idea IDs, actual execution evidence, and permitted outcome sources. For published social results, follow [analytics-input.md](analytics-input.md) first. For outbound, ads, directories, or community work, use the configured source or a compact founder report. Do not demand a CRM connection, private messages, or social metrics for an ICP-only review.

## Separate execution from market response

Record what ran, when, for which segment and rule version, with what offer/channel, and how much founder time and money it used. Preserve denominators: researched prospects, approached prospects, delivered messages when known, relevant replies, and conversions are different populations. Provider acceptance is not delivery; a copied draft is not a send; silence does not prove lack of fit when delivery is unknown.

Keep distribution, engagement, business learning, and product value separate. Impressions, clicks, or replies do not establish activation, repeat use, or payment. Define the first useful product action for each experiment before declaring activation. Report founder attribution versus instrumented attribution and unknowns; do not invent post-level credit for aggregate results.

Compare results at similar observation windows and with relevant context: segment, offer, channel, exposure, and effort. Sparse samples support learning questions, not conversion forecasts or causal certainty. A successful message does not validate every weight in a score. Missing execution or insufficient exposure should usually produce an inconclusive assessment.

## Decide what to change

For each executed play, recommend `continue`, `revise`, `stop`, or `inconclusive`, citing evidence and the next discriminating observation. Consider whether the problem lies in source quality, ICP fit, timing, offer, message, reach/delivery, or product experience. Avoid changing all of them together and then claiming to know what worked.

Feed findings back into the ICP, qualification rules, signal definitions, and play selection. Record counterexamples as well as apparent wins. When a rule changes, retain its prior version and why it changed in the run so old results remain interpretable. Update observed facts directly; distinguish proposed strategy changes from founder decisions. Preserve the user's explicit selections without asking for the same decision again.

## Learn from writing when text was published

Match the draft to final text and classify substantive edits as substance, structure, tone, or wording. Note removals as well as additions. One edit is a clue, not a universal rule. Compare external technique trials with channel, audience, topic, post age, and reach context; unknown exposure prevents a confident ranking.

Put proposed voice changes in `style.md` under `Proposed updates`; promote them only after founder acceptance. Keep public links and de-identified assessments, not private customer messages. Use the compact record below for meaningful strategy or style proposals:

```text
Proposal: one narrow change
Evidence: source/run/play ID, date, observed outcome, limitations
Confidence: tentative | repeated
Founder decision: pending | accepted | rejected
```

End with what was learned, what remains unknown, the budget/time position, and one justified next experiment or research step. If no results exist, describe the evidence still needed rather than claiming the system has learned what performs best.
